How it's built

A verification pipeline designed to ground public accountability in cryptographic proof.

Keenable finds sources.

Searches contemporaneous SF municipal records, agency dashboards, and local news archives.

Archil sandboxes keep the advocates apart and store the evidence.

Affirming and contradicting advocates run in separated processes to prevent cross-contamination.

Code decides.

Seven deterministic checks verify hashes, dates, and quotes. The arbitrator makes no model calls.

PROJECT ORIGIN & PURPOSE

Tamper-evident civic accountability for San Francisco

SF Promises was created to bring verifiable, cryptographically grounded transparency to San Francisco public commitments. Originating at the Archil Hackathon (November 2026) and awarded Best Use of Keenable, the pipeline replaces subjective commentary with deterministic code checks against saved, hashed municipal documents.

Technical & Audit Specifications  ▸

Verification Architecture

  • Archil Cloud Disks: Separated sandboxes isolate affirming and contradicting investigators with zero cross-talk.
  • Keenable Temporal API: Strict contemporaneous web discovery across allowlisted SF municipal and news domains.
  • Cryptographic Digests: SHA-256 fingerprints lock primary document bytes upon ingest to prevent retrofitting.
  • Deterministic Arbitrator: Seven rule-based checks decide outcomes with zero model calls at arbitration time.
  • Durability Engine: Automated re-verification monitors source URLs for status or text drift.

Open Data & Heritage

  • Hugging Face Dataset: Open relational SQLite DB, 51-column dataset, and standardized 16-column master table.
  • OpenPromises: Open commitment verification principles.
  • kept.vote: Civic promise accountability.
  • CouncilHound: Municipal council tracking.
What this can't tell you ↓

SF Promises verifies whether specific public commitments were fulfilled as written in verifiable municipal documents. Here is what deterministic checks cannot capture:

  • Private negotiations: Verbal agreements, closed-door council negotiations, or undocumented promises made without public record.
  • Intent & motivation: We track outcomes (did the beds open?), not whether an official tried hard or faced unexpected political resistance.
  • Nuanced capital delays: Major infrastructure projects face complex supply-chain or legal delays that binary checks record strictly as delayed.
  • Append-only corrections: When ground truth changes (like a store closing after opening), the ledger re-checks every 90 days and appends corrections rather than erasing history.
