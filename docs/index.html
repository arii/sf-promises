How to read the verdicts

What counts as a promise: a specific, attributable public statement that commits a named person or organization to a concrete action or outcome. Vague aspirations and opinions are excluded. This site tracks 54 such commitments across City Hall, SFO Airport, transit, and sports.

Kept
The documented outcome matches what was promised.
Broken
Sources show the commitment was not delivered, or was abandoned or missed its deadline.
In Progress
Work is underway and the deadline has not passed.
Undecided
Sources are not yet sufficient to judge either way. This is not the same as Broken: nothing documents a failure.
Reversed
The promise was kept at one point and later undone or closed. The verdict history shows both steps.

A verdict describes the outcome as of its date. The history of when a promise was made and checked is shown separately in each record, so a past verdict is never rewritten silently.

Methodology and limitations

  • Small, curated sample. The 181 commitments were selected from sources that could be saved and hashed. They are not a census of every promise made in San Francisco, so the Kept and Broken shares should not be read as a city-wide success rate.
  • Evidence-limited. A verdict reflects the saved sources available at the time. Missing documentation leads to Undecided, not Broken.
  • Dated. Each record shows when the promise was made and when its checks last ran. The dataset was last checked on Nov 14, 2026.
What this can't tell you ↓

SF Promises verifies whether specific public commitments were fulfilled as written in verifiable municipal documents. Here is what deterministic checks cannot capture:

  • Private negotiations: Verbal agreements, closed-door council negotiations, or undocumented promises made without public record.
  • Intent & motivation: We track outcomes (did the beds open?), not whether an official tried hard or faced unexpected political resistance.
  • Nuanced capital delays: Major infrastructure projects face complex supply-chain or legal delays that binary checks record strictly as delayed.
  • Append-only corrections: When ground truth changes (like a store closing after opening), the ledger re-checks every 90 days and appends corrections rather than erasing history.

How it's built

A verification pipeline designed to ground public accountability in archived documents and automated checks.

Keenable finds sources.

Searches contemporaneous SF municipal records, agency dashboards, and local news archives.

Independent searches archive their own evidence.

Affirming and contradicting searches run independently to collect comprehensive documentation.

Code decides.

The same seven checks run on every record, without language-model calls at decision time.

PROJECT ORIGIN & PURPOSE

Civic accountability for San Francisco

SF Promises tracks public commitments made by San Francisco leaders against archived municipal records, agency dashboards, and contemporaneous news sources. Built at the Archil Hackathon (November 2026), the system tests published statements against verifiable milestone dates and source documents.

Technical & Audit Specifications

Verification Architecture

  • Local SQLite workspace: Content-addressed sources and bounded worker processes; not VM security boundaries.
  • Keenable Temporal API: Web discovery across allowlisted SF municipal and news archives.
  • Cryptographic Digests: SHA-256 hashes lock source document bytes upon ingest.
  • Deterministic Arbitrator: Seven rule-based checks decide outcomes with zero model calls at arbitration time.
  • Re-verification: Periodic crawls detect milestone completions and status changes.

Open Data & Heritage

  • Hugging Face Dataset: Open relational SQLite DB, 51-column dataset, and standardized 16-column master table.
  • OpenPromises: Open commitment verification principles.
  • kept.vote: Civic promise accountability.
  • CouncilHound: Municipal council tracking.
